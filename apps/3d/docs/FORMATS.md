# Formats and measurement behavior

Shenzhen 3D imports one supported model or drawing at a time. When opening a group of files, the first supported model is selected; other selected files are available as companions. Select an OBJ with its MTL/textures, or an external glTF with its BIN/textures, in the same operation. The importer resolves companions by filename, so use unique companion basenames. Missing companions generate notes or an import error, depending on what the loader needs.

The mesh/vector baseline and fixture scope are recorded in [format validation](FORMAT_VALIDATION.md). Implemented loaders can accept additional variants beyond these fixtures, but format support is not a promise of complete feature coverage. Animation playback, CAD authoring history, constraints and proprietary application metadata are not imported. Native SolidWorks (`.sldprt`, `.sldasm`), CATIA, Creo, Inventor and similar authoring formats require export to STEP/IGES/BREP or a supported mesh format.

## Large STEP assemblies

STEP files of 16 MB or more are read one product at a time, so file size is no longer limited by the CAD kernel's 2 GB memory. A large KiCad assembly (about 100 MB) opens with a first view after about 2 s and fully in 9–15 s on an Apple M5 Max; the renderer peaks near 1 GB and settles near 0.5 GB. The limit is now the largest single product: one product must still fit in 2 GB of kernel memory, which corresponds to roughly a 30 MB product inside the file. Products drawn only as curves (silkscreen or outline sketches) are listed in the assembly tree but have no geometry, as before. Exact feature queries read a product's exact geometry on first use; most take well under a second, very complex single solids several seconds. Files whose assembly uses mapped items, context-dependent styles or other uncommon structures are read whole, as before. Details and measurements are in [PERFORMANCE.md](PERFORMANCE.md#large-step-assemblies).

## Drawings

The drawing renderer handles lines, circles/arcs, lightweight/2D/3D polylines with supported bulges, ellipses, splines, block inserts, supported dimension blocks, text, simple filled faces and points. It reports skipped/unsupported entities. Hatches, embedded CAD solids and arbitrary custom objects are not a full-fidelity drafting path. Drawing text uses a substitute font; wrapping, alignment and formatting can differ. Supported lines/arcs/closed polylines carry analytic length or area metadata; sampled curves retain their stated approximation limits.

## Choosing a measurement

Point mode measures the selected geometry positions: two points produce straight-line Euclidean separation and signed axis components; three points produce the angle at the middle point. Snapping helps select nearby geometry vertices. A screen-picked mesh point is limited by the imported tessellation.

Feature mode queries retained CAD edges, faces and vertices when available. The application presents supported lengths, areas and analytic radii/diameters, plus minimum distance, applicable feature angles and normal thickness. These are kernel queries against the original geometry, so changing CAD tessellation quality affects the display rather than those exact queries. A particular feature pair may not support a requested angle, center or thickness; the interface reports unavailable cases.

The distance reference can use minimum separation, picked positions, or centers/centroids. Centers come from supported analytic features; CAD face centroids and edge endpoint midpoints provide appropriate fallbacks where available. Mesh circular-boundary fitting can also provide a center. These references are different quantities; center separation is not clearance between surfaces.

Meshes do not acquire CAD topology. Connected coplanar triangle patches provide approximate area and boundary perimeter; sufficiently sampled circular boundaries can provide fitted radius/diameter. Degenerate, non-manifold, sparse or very large inputs can fall back to triangle-level properties or omit an estimate. These values describe the tessellation and are labeled approximate.

Surface mode computes an approximate route across a connected mesh on one part. It uses triangle edges plus the actual endpoint anchors, so it can overestimate a continuous surface geodesic. It does not route between separate parts or disconnected components.

## Units, placement and viewing

Display units support mm, cm, m, inches, feet and micrometers. **Source units & scale** overrides how model coordinates are interpreted; a positive custom scale multiplies distances and squared scale applies to areas. Exports include the selected interpretation. Check known dimensions before relying on an assumed-unit mesh. KiCad WRL files can use 0.1 inch per coordinate; the panel supplies a shortcut for that convention.

Measurements use assembly coordinates. Explode, opacity, hidden parts and section planes assist inspection; a section plane clips the view without cutting the CAD model or generating new exact section geometry. Saved measurements and annotations belong to the current session/model. Share a portable `.sz3d` project to retain the model, notes and measurements together, or export a CSV/JSON measurement report. See [review files](REVIEW.md) for archive behavior and limits. PNG screenshots capture the viewport; they are not dimensioned engineering drawings.
